# User Persona Findings

The full-history analysis of 16,412,862 negative-review records revealed distinct complaint profiles across the four segments. Hardcore accounted for 37.63% of records, Dropout for 7.17%, New Blood for 7.45%, and Nobody for 47.74%. These are player–game review records rather than unique players. All reported complaint rates use every record in the corresponding segment as the denominator, including records with no flagged complaints.

Hardcore reviews showed a distinctive emphasis on the social environment and ongoing service. Although Technical was the most prevalent domain within this segment (25.48%), Hardcore had the highest Social (21.14%) and Service (19.12%) prevalence among the four groups. Its Top 5 factors were Cheating and failed enforcement (12.16%), Perceived maintenance neglect (11.32%), Broken functions (8.50%), Launch and crash failures (7.42%), and Content breadth and availability (6.23%). Cheating and failed enforcement exceeded the overall historical prevalence by 6.59 percentage points. This profile suggests that fairness and continued maintenance deserve investigation among reviews combining higher prior playtime with higher predicted later activity.

Dropout reviews concentrated on gameplay, content, and progression. Gameplay complaints appeared in 39.76% of records and Content complaints in 33.20%, both the highest rates across segments. The Top 5 factors were Interaction and play rhythm (34.20%), Content breadth and availability (21.80%), Story and characters (16.53%), Effort, rewards, and loss (16.16%), and Difficulty and encounter fairness (12.17%). Interaction and play rhythm exceeded the overall baseline by 21.48 percentage points. Dropout also had the highest proportion of records with at least one retained complaint flag (76.59%) and the highest mean number of flagged factors per record (1.77). This indicates broader recorded dissatisfaction with the playing experience among reviews combining higher prior playtime with lower predicted later activity.

New Blood reviews were dominated by technical reliability concerns. Technical complaints appeared in 42.20% of records, the highest rate among the four groups. Its Top 5 factors were Launch and crash failures (17.71%), Performance and loading (10.65%), Broken functions (9.07%), Connection failures (8.52%), and Perceived maintenance neglect (7.62%). Launch and crash failures exceeded the overall baseline by 9.56 percentage points. Four of its five leading factors were technical, suggesting that stability, performance, and access warrant investigation among reviews with lower prior playtime and higher predicted later activity.

Nobody was the largest segment and had a more mixed complaint profile. Technical (22.59%) and Gameplay (20.79%) were its leading domains, while its User experience prevalence (12.67%) was the highest across segments. Its Top 5 factors were Interaction and play rhythm (17.28%), Content breadth and availability (10.46%), Price and value (7.78%), Launch and crash failures (7.78%), and Broken functions (7.63%). Price and value ranked slightly above Launch and crash failures using unrounded rates. The combination of gameplay, content, value, and technical concerns suggests several possible sources of dissatisfaction among reviews with lower prior playtime and lower predicted later activity.

These findings support investigating different issues across segments: fairness and maintenance for Hardcore, gameplay and content for Dropout, technical reliability for New Blood, and gameplay fit, value, and usability for Nobody. They are hypotheses for further review and intervention testing. Complaint flags are NLP estimates, and NLP features also contribute to the model defining the groups. The comparisons are retrospective, include in-sample training predictions, and do not adjust for game, language, or time composition. They therefore describe associations and do not establish actual churn, causal drivers, or the effectiveness of a proposed intervention.
